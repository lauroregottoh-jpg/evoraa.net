# 17.1_Assessment_Philosophy.md

**Version:** 1.0 (MVP)

---

# Purpose

This document defines the philosophy behind the assessment system used by KELIA.

It serves as the foundation for every questionnaire, score and compatibility calculation.

---

# Vision

KELIA does not aim to decide whether two people should marry.

Its purpose is to help members discover profiles with a higher probability of relational and spiritual compatibility.

The assessments are decision-support tools, not decision-makers.

---

# Objectives

The assessments are designed to:

- Improve match quality
- Reduce incompatible introductions
- Encourage self-awareness
- Help members understand their relational tendencies

---

# Guiding Principles

## Principle 1

Assessments are informative, not judgmental.

---

## Principle 2

No profile is considered "better" than another.

Different profiles have different strengths.

---

## Principle 3

Results should remain simple.

The MVP avoids complex psychological reports.

Users receive:

- a profile summary
- key strengths
- areas of attention
- compatibility percentage

---

## Principle 4

Assessments should be quick.

Target completion time:

8 to 10 minutes.

Maximum:

35 questions across all assessments.

---

# Assessment Structure

The MVP includes three assessments.

1. Personality Assessment
   - 12 questions

2. Spiritual Compatibility Assessment
   - 10 questions

3. Relationship Compatibility Assessment
   - 12 questions

Total target:

34 questions.

---

# Scoring Philosophy

Each assessment produces:

- a normalized score
- a dominant profile
- structured data for the matching engine

Scores are not displayed in raw form to other users.

---

# Ethics

The platform must never:

- label users as "good" or "bad"
- judge spiritual value
- predict marriage success
- replace pastoral or professional guidance

---

# Future Evolution

Future versions may include:

- deeper assessments
- premium reports
- AI-assisted explanations

These features are intentionally excluded from the MVP.
